5a:T791,Adding a picture to a PDF that already exists — a logo on a letterhead, a stamp, a photo dropped into a report — is something the format is awkward about. A PDF page has no notion of moving anything: everything on it is drawn at fixed coordinates, so adding a picture means drawing it in too. That is what this tool does. Drop a PDF, choose a PNG or JPEG, and the page you are looking at is rendered with PDF.js and given a marker you can drag. Click or drag anywhere on the page to put the image's top-left corner there, or type exact percentages into the X and Y fields — the fields and the drag are the same two numbers, so the placement works from the keyboard alone and the marker cannot disagree with the file you download. Width is a percentage of the page's own width and the height follows the image's proportions, so nothing is stretched. Opacity thins the image, and Turn swings it counter-clockwise about its own bottom-left corner. You choose which pages get it: all of them, the first, the last, or one page you name. You also choose whether it is drawn over the page's content or under it, which is what a background letterhead wants — though a page with a filled background of its own will hide anything placed underneath. Three things worth knowing before you rely on the output. The picture is drawn into the page's content rather than added as an annotation object, so it is permanent: nobody can click it in a reader and drag it away, and neither can you. An image whose EXIF Orientation tag is not 1 has to be redrawn upright before it is embedded, because PDF readers ignore that tag, and for a JPEG that costs one re-encode — the canvas says so when it happens. A page the file stores with its own rotation is handled: the marker and the download agree at 0, 90, 180 and 270 degrees, which is pinned by a test over all four.
