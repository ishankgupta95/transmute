3c:T8f1,Some PDFs are large because of how they are built, and some are large because of what is inside them. This tool is for the second kind. It walks every image the document embeds, decodes the ones stored as JPEG, redraws them and writes them back at a lower JPEG quality — and, when you give it a target size, at smaller pixel dimensions as well. That is a lossy edit and it cannot be undone, so keep your original. What it never touches is the text and the vector artwork: those come out exactly as they went in, which is why a report with photographs in it stays sharp everywhere the photographs are not. There are two ways to drive it. Quality offers three presets and makes a single pass at the one you pick. Target size takes a number in KB and searches for it — quality first, then progressively smaller image dimensions, rebuilding the document each time and keeping the best-looking result that still fits under your number — the highest quality that lands under it, not the smallest file it can make. When nothing fits, you get the closest size it managed and a plain statement that the target was not reached, rather than a file quietly larger than you asked for. The more interesting half is what it refuses to do. Only images stored as JPEG are eligible. An image held as JPEG 2000, CCITT fax, JBIG2 or a deflated bitmap is written back byte for byte as it came in, and so is anything carrying a transparency mask, anything with its own decode array or predictor parameters, anything in CMYK or an indexed or ICC-tagged colour space, and anything that is not eight bits per channel. Rewriting those needs codecs and colour handling this tool deliberately does not carry, and a wrong guess prints an inverted or falsely coloured page. Every skipped image is counted, grouped by reason and shown on screen, because a compressor that silently does nothing is worse than one that says so. There is a per-image guard too: if the re-encode comes back bigger than the image it replaced, the original image stays. Each image is processed once however many pages draw it, and the finished document is written back with its objects packed into object streams.
