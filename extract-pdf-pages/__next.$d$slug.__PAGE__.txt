3b:T8cb,Extracting pages is two different jobs wearing one name, and this tool does both. Drop a PDF and you get a deck of page thumbnails; click the pages you want, or type them into the RANGE field as something like 1-3, 5, 8-10 — the deck and the field are the same selection in two shapes, so typing updates the tiles and clicking rewrites the text. Select all and Deselect all sit in the rail next to them. Then pick the output mode. One PDF gives you a single document holding the selected pages in their original order, which is the ordinary case. One per page gives you a separate PDF for each page you picked, named after the page number it came from — pick pages 3 and 9 and you get page-3.pdf and page-9.pdf, delivered together as a ZIP. Select exactly one page in that mode and there is nothing to zip, so the file saves on its own instead, and the readout says so before you press anything. The pages themselves are grafted across whole by pdf-lib rather than redrawn: fonts, embedded images and vector art come over exactly as they were, annotations travel with the page they sit on, and nothing is re-encoded or rasterised at any point. What does not come across is the layer that belongs to the document rather than to any page — the outline tree, the interactive form, named destinations and page labels. So an extracted file starts with no bookmarks, and if the source was a fillable form the result still draws every field and whatever was typed into it but is no longer a form: the boxes cannot be filled in or read back by a form tool. Front matter numbered i, ii, iii comes back as 1, 2, 3 for the same reason. Internal cross-references are followed through rather than left to fend for themselves: a link whose target page is one of the ones you picked is repointed at it and still works, and a link to a page you left behind has its destination stripped, so it does nothing instead of quietly jumping to the front of the document. A link that named its target rather than pointing straight at it goes dead either way, because the table of names is part of the same document layer that does not travel. All of that is equally true of merging and splitting here.
