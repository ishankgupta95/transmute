About Video to Images

Turning a video into a folder of pictures is the sort of thing that normally means installing FFmpeg and learning a filtergraph. This does it in the browser instead, and it does it without FFmpeg at all: the clip is played through your browser's own video decoder, every frame it presents is drawn onto a canvas, and the canvas encodes it as PNG, JPEG or WebP. No conversion engine is downloaded and nothing is uploaded. There are two ways to choose which frames you get. Every N seconds takes the first frame at the very start of the clip and then one at each interval until the end, which is what you want for a contact sheet or a set of stills to pick from. Fixed count spaces the frames evenly and takes each one from the middle of its slice, so the first picture is a picture rather than the black leader most clips open on. One run saves at most 300 frames, and that ceiling is stated before you click as well as after: the page shows how many frames the settings ask for, how many will be saved, and where in the clip the run stops. It never quietly hands back a shorter set. Two other honesty notes. A video only carries pictures where it carries pictures, so asking for one every half-second from a clip shot at one frame per second cannot invent the ones in between — the saved frame is stamped with the time it really came from, and the run reports how many of the moments you asked for landed inside a picture already saved. And a browser that cannot write WebP from a canvas falls back to PNG, in which case the files are named .png and the page says so. The output is always a ZIP, even for a single frame, with each file named by its position and the exact moment it came from so 300 files still unzip in order.
