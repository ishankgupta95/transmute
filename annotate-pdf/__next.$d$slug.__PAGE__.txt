3b:T7da,Annotate PDF puts three kinds of mark on a page you choose: a translucent highlight block, an outlined box, and a short text note. The page is rendered with PDF.js exactly as a reader will see it, and you place marks on that picture — drag to set a rectangle, click to move one, or type its position and size into the X, Y, Width and Height fields. Those fields are the same four numbers the drag writes, so the page and the rail can never disagree and nothing here needs a mouse. Two things this page cannot do, and they are the two people assume it can. It does not select text: there is no text layer under the preview and the engine has no model of where the words sit, so a highlight is a coloured rectangle at the spot you chose, not a highlight that finds a line and follows it across a column break. And the marks are painted into the page's own content rather than attached as PDF annotation objects, so a reader shows no comment to click, no author, no reply and no delete. That makes them permanent in normal use — taking one out again means editing the page. If you want something a colleague can answer, this is the wrong tool. The rest is detail worth knowing before you rely on it. A highlight is a flat translucent block laid over the page, not a multiply blend, so the text underneath comes through tinted, and at full opacity it covers that text completely. Notes are drawn in Helvetica and can only carry the characters the standard PDF fonts cover, which is Latin letters, digits and punctuation including accents, curly quotes and the euro sign; Chinese, Cyrillic, Greek, Arabic and emoji are refused before you press the button rather than after. A note is never wrapped for you, so press Enter where a line should break, and be aware that extra lines land twenty-four points below the one before whatever size you pick. Only the page you marked is changed, and only by adding to it.
