3c:T770,Most tools called a PDF editor are really PDF markers, and this page says so on the way in: the text already in your document cannot be edited here. A PDF page is not a document in the word-processor sense. It is a list of drawing instructions — this glyph, this font, this size, at these coordinates — with no paragraphs, no line boxes and nothing to reflow. Neither pdf-lib nor PDF.js, the two libraries behind this page, can find the sentence a letter belongs to, change it and re-lay-out what follows. Adobe's own free tier cannot do it either. What you get instead are five things a PDF genuinely can do, and all five are exact. Add text anywhere on a page, in Helvetica or Helvetica Bold, at whatever size and colour you choose. Place a PNG or JPEG — a logo, a stamp, a photo — sized as a share of the page width, so nothing is stretched. Paint a whiteout rectangle over something you want out of sight. Draw freehand with a pointer. And delete whole pages you do not want. The page you are working on is rendered exactly as a reader will see it, marks are placed on that picture, and every mark remembers the page it belongs to, so you can work through several pages and apply the lot in one pass. Placement is expressed as percentages of the page as displayed, and those number fields are the mark — dragging on the page writes the same numbers — so text, images and whiteout boxes can be placed to the point from a keyboard alone. Freehand is the one exception, and this page says so rather than pretending. Two limits are worth reading twice. Whiteout is paint, not redaction: the covered text is still in the file and still copyable by anything that reads the page. And every mark is drawn into the page's own content rather than attached as an annotation object, so no reader will offer you a comment to click or a Delete.
